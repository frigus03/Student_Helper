Subject: Biology 120 - Cellular Biology
Topic: Cell Signaling and Signal Transduction Pathways

Three Stages of Cell Signaling:
1. Reception: The target cell detects a signaling molecule (ligand) binding to a receptor protein on the cell surface or inside the cell.
2. Transduction: The binding alters the receptor protein, triggering a cascade of intracellular molecular interactions (signal transduction pathway).
3. Response: The transduced signal initiates a specific cellular activity, such as gene expression, enzymatic activation, or cellular rearrangement.

Major Receptor Types:
- G-Protein Coupled Receptors (GPCRs): Work with G-proteins that bind GDP (inactive) or GTP (active).
- Receptor Tyrosine Kinases (RTKs): Possess enzymatic activity; dimerize upon ligand binding and autophosphorylate tyrosine residues.
- Ion Channel Receptors: Ligand-gated channels that open or close in response to specific chemical signals.

Second Messengers:
Small, non-protein, water-soluble molecules or ions that spread rapidly by diffusion (e.g., Cyclic AMP [cAMP], Calcium ions [Ca2+], IP3).
